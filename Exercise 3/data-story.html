TV ENERGY DATA STORY

Bigger Screen, Bigger Energy Use?

Exploring how television screen size and screen technology relate to annual energy consumption.

01

WHO ARE WE TALKING TO?

Target Audience

This data story is designed for consumers who are planning to purchase a television and want to understand how their choice may affect electricity consumption.

Rather than focusing on technical specifications, the visualisations highlight two factors that are easy for consumers to understand: screen size and screen technology.

What does our audience want to know?

Does choosing a larger television mean using more energy, and does the type of screen technology make a difference?

02

STORYBOARD 1

Does a Bigger TV Use More Energy?

First, let's look at the relationship between television screen size and annual energy consumption.

Figure 1. Television screen size compared with labelled annual energy consumption.

WHAT DOES THE DATA SHOW?

Bigger screens generally consume more energy.

The scatter plot shows a clear upward pattern. Smaller televisions are mostly concentrated at lower annual energy consumption levels, while larger televisions generally appear at much higher levels.

However, televisions with similar screen sizes do not always consume the same amount of energy. The spread of points becomes particularly noticeable among larger TVs.

But screen size does not explain everything.

Why can televisions of a similar size still have different energy consumption?

One possible factor is the screen technology used by the television.

03

STORYBOARD 2

Does Screen Technology Make a Difference?

We can investigate further by comparing LCD, LCD (LED) and OLED televisions across different screen-size categories.

Figure 2. Average annual energy consumption by television size category and screen technology.

WHAT DOES THE DATA SHOW?

Screen technology matters, but size remains important.

Large televisions have the highest average energy consumption across all three screen technologies. This supports the pattern identified in the first visualisation.

Differences can also be seen between LCD, LCD (LED) and OLED televisions within the same size category. However, no single technology consistently has the lowest energy consumption across every size group.

This suggests that consumers should consider the energy performance of the individual television rather than choosing a TV based only on its display technology.

THE BIG PICTURE

What Should Consumers Take Away?

01

Size Matters

Larger television screens are generally associated with greater annual energy consumption.

02

Technology Matters Too

TVs of similar sizes can still have different energy consumption depending on their technology and design.

03

Check the Individual TV

Consumers should compare the energy consumption or energy rating of individual models before making a purchase.

The key message: A bigger screen will generally require more energy, but screen size alone does not determine a television's energy performance.
